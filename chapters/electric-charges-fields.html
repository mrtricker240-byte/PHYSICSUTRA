Concept Explorer

Understand the fundamental ideas of electrostatics.

01 · FUNDAMENTALS

Electric Charge

Electric charge is a basic property of matter responsible for electrical interactions.

  • Two types: positive and negative.
  • Like charges repel; unlike charges attract.
  • SI unit: coulomb (C).
  • Charge is conserved in an isolated system.
  • Charge is quantised: q = ne, where n is an integer.
e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C

Magnitude of the elementary charge.

02 · ELECTROSTATIC FORCE

Coulomb's Law

The electrostatic force between two point charges depends on their charges and separation.

F = k |q₁q₂| / r²

In vacuum, k = 1/(4πε₀) ≈ 9 × 10⁹ N m²/C².

  • The force acts along the line joining the charges.
  • It is repulsive for like charges and attractive for unlike charges.
  • In a medium, the force depends on its permittivity.
03 · SUPERPOSITION

Principle of Superposition

The net electrostatic force on a charge is the vector sum of forces exerted by all other charges.

F⃗net = Σ F⃗i

Add force vectors, not just their magnitudes.

04 · ELECTRIC FIELD

Electric Field

The electric field at a point is the force experienced per unit positive test charge.

E⃗ = F⃗ / q₀

SI unit: N/C or V/m.

For a point charge, the magnitude of the field is proportional to |q| and inversely proportional to r².

E = k |q| / r²
05 · FIELD LINES

Electric Field Lines

  • They start on positive charges and end on negative charges, or extend to infinity.
  • The tangent at a point gives the field direction.
  • Closer lines indicate a stronger field in a field-line diagram.
  • Electrostatic field lines never intersect.
  • They do not form closed loops in electrostatics.
06 · DIPOLE

Electric Dipole

An electric dipole consists of two equal and opposite charges separated by a small distance.

p = q × 2a

Dipole moment magnitude; direction is from negative to positive charge.

SI unit: C m. For a uniform electric field, the torque magnitude is τ = pE sin θ.

07 · FLUX

Electric Flux

For a uniform electric field passing through a flat surface, flux depends on the field, area and angle.

Φ = EA cos θ

θ is the angle between the field and the area vector.

08 · GAUSS'S LAW

Gauss's Law

The net electric flux through a closed surface equals the enclosed charge divided by the permittivity of free space.

∮ E⃗ · dA⃗ = Qenclosed / ε₀

It is especially useful when the charge distribution has spherical, cylindrical or planar symmetry.

Solved Examples

Learn how to apply the formulas step by step.

EXAMPLE 01

Force between two point charges

Two charges of magnitudes 2 μC and 3 μC are separated by 10 cm in vacuum. Find the force magnitude.

Step 1: Convert to SI units.
q₁ = 2 × 10⁻⁶ C, q₂ = 3 × 10⁻⁶ C, r = 0.1 m.

Step 2: Apply Coulomb's law.
F = (9 × 10⁹)(2 × 10⁻⁶)(3 × 10⁻⁶)/(0.1)²

Answer: F = 5.4 N.
The force is attractive for opposite signs and repulsive for like signs.
EXAMPLE 02

Electric field due to a point charge

Calculate the field magnitude at a distance of 0.3 m from a point charge of 2 μC in vacuum.

Formula: E = k|q|/r²

q = 2 × 10⁻⁶ C and r = 0.3 m.

E = (9 × 10⁹)(2 × 10⁻⁶)/(0.3)²
E = 18,000/0.09

Answer: E = 2 × 10⁵ N/C.
For a positive source charge, the field points outward.
